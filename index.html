01

About the Project

Building a more transparent and verifiable question-answering system for Turkish documents.

TR-RAG is a graduation research project focused on developing a Turkish question-answering assistant using Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG). Rather than relying solely on a language model's internal knowledge, the proposed system retrieves relevant information from documents before generating an answer.

The system aims to provide answers supported by identifiable source passages, allowing users to verify the information against the original documents. When the available evidence is insufficient, the assistant is designed to acknowledge this limitation instead of generating unsupported information.

A central focus of the research is Turkish language processing. By comparing different retrieval and generation configurations, the project will investigate how Turkish linguistic characteristics influence retrieval effectiveness, answer quality, and overall system reliability.

Document-grounded answers

Answers are generated using relevant passages retrieved from Turkish document collections.

Verifiable citations

Generated responses aim to include references that allow users to trace information back to its source.

Evidence-aware responses

The system is designed to recognize insufficient evidence and avoid unsupported answers.

Turkish-focused research

The project evaluates RAG methods with particular attention to the linguistic properties of Turkish.

02

Why Turkish?

Investigating how the linguistic structure of Turkish affects retrieval-augmented question answering.

Turkish presents distinctive challenges for natural language processing due to its agglutinative morphology, productive suffixation, and flexible sentence structure. These characteristics can influence how questions and document passages are represented, matched, and interpreted by RAG systems.

A question and its supporting document passage may express related information using different inflected forms of the same word. Consequently, retrieval methods must account for variation beyond exact keyword matches.

TR-RAG investigates how different document processing, embedding, retrieval, and re-ranking approaches perform when applied to Turkish text.

01 / Morphology

Agglutinative Language Structure

Turkish words can contain multiple suffixes expressing grammatical relationships. The resulting word forms may complicate lexical matching and influence how semantic representations are learned.

02 / Retrieval

Lexical and Semantic Variation

Relevant questions and document passages may use different surface forms. The project will explore keyword-based, semantic, and hybrid retrieval approaches to address these variations.

03 / Representation

Turkish-Capable Embeddings

Different embedding models may represent Turkish vocabulary, inflection, and sentence meaning with varying effectiveness. Comparing these models is an important component of the proposed research.

04 / Evaluation

Language-Specific Performance

Retrieval and generation quality will be examined using Turkish questions and supporting documents, helping identify effective configurations and recurring challenges.

Example · Turkish Morphology
kitaplarımızdan

kitap (book) + lar (plural) + ımız (first-person plural possessive) + dan (ablative case). A single Turkish word can encode information expressed through several words in English. This illustrates why morphological variation is relevant to retrieval and text representation.

03

Method

Multiple RAG configurations will be implemented and systematically evaluated to identify effective approaches for Turkish document-based question answering.

  1. Document chunking Fixed-size, sentence-based, and overlapping chunking strategies will be compared to examine how document segmentation influences retrieval quality.
  2. Turkish embedding models Different Turkish-capable embedding models will be evaluated for their ability to represent Turkish questions and document passages.
  3. Vector databases Document embeddings will be indexed and retrieved using different vector database configurations.
  4. Hybrid search Keyword-based retrieval using BM25 will be combined with semantic similarity search to investigate whether complementary retrieval signals improve relevant passage selection.
  5. Re-ranking Retrieved passages will be re-scored to prioritize evidence that is most relevant to the user's question.
  6. Answer generation Open-source language models will generate Turkish answers from retrieved context, accompanied by source references when sufficient evidence is available.
04

Evaluation

Comparing answer quality, evidence reliability, and efficiency across RAG configurations.

The proposed configurations will be evaluated using a common set of Turkish questions and reference documents. The evaluation will focus on the correctness of generated answers, their relationship to the retrieved evidence, and the computational efficiency of the system.

Metric What it measures
Answer accuracy Whether the generated answer is correct according to the information contained in the reference documents.
Source consistency Whether cited passages actually support the claims made in the generated answer.
Hallucination rate How frequently generated answers include information that is not supported by the available evidence.
Response time The time required to retrieve relevant information and generate a response.

The results will be analyzed to identify which approaches perform effectively on Turkish-language content and to examine trade-offs between answer reliability, retrieval quality, and response time.
